# 05 — Contract Mockups (Fill-in-the-Blank Templates)

> ⚠️ **TEMPLATE MOCKUPS — NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** These are educational skeletons to teach you the *structure* and *levers* of partner contracts and to use as drafting starting points. **Have qualified counsel review and localize anything before use.** Benchmark numbers are directional (mostly vendor-sourced, no audited methodology). Fields to complete are shown as `[like this]`.

**The six documents a channel program needs:**
1. Referral Partner Agreement
2. Reseller / Channel Partner Agreement
3. Co-Sell / Strategic Alliance Agreement (+ Rules of Engagement)
4. Technology / Integration Partnership Agreement
5. Mutual NDA
6. Partner Program Terms / Rules of Engagement (program document)

**Golden rule used throughout:** keep the master agreement short and put all **volatile commercial terms** (pricing, discounts, MDF %, tier thresholds, SLAs) in **Exhibits/Schedules/Addenda** so they can be updated without reopening the contract.

---

## 1. Referral Partner Agreement

*Partner introduces leads; you close, bill, and support; partner earns a commission. Lightest-touch — the partner has no authority to quote, contract, or support.*

```
REFERRAL PARTNER AGREEMENT

This Referral Partner Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of [Effective Date]
by and between [Company Legal Name], [entity type & address] ("Company"),
and [Partner Legal Name], [entity type & address] ("Partner").

1. DEFINITIONS
   "Referral" — a prospective customer introduced by Partner and submitted per Section 3.
   "Qualified Referral" — a Referral that (a) is net-new (not already in Company's
      pipeline or a customer), (b) meets Company's ICP [define], and (c) is accepted
      by Company under Section 3.
   "Referral Fee" — the commission in Schedule A.
   "Eligibility Window" — [180] days from Company's acceptance of a Referral.

2. APPOINTMENT
   Company appoints Partner as a [non-exclusive] referral partner in [territory/vertical
   or "worldwide"]. Partner is an independent contractor with NO authority to bind,
   quote, negotiate, price, or make representations on Company's behalf.

3. REFERRAL SUBMISSION & ACCEPTANCE
   Partner submits Referrals via [portal / form / email] including [company, contact,
   use case]. Company may accept or reject any Referral in its sole discretion and will
   notify Partner within [5] business days. Only accepted Referrals are eligible.

4. REFERRAL FEE  (see Schedule A)
   Company pays Partner [__]% of [Net New first-year ARR / first invoice value] for each
   Qualified Referral that becomes a paying customer within the Eligibility Window.
   [First transaction only / Recurring for __ months]. Fees exclude [taxes, discounts,
   third-party pass-through, refunds].

5. PAYMENT TERMS
   Fees accrue when [Company receives customer payment]. Company pays within [30] days
   of [the end of the month in which the triggering event occurs], via [method], in
   [currency]. Clawback applies on customer refund or non-payment within [__] days.

6. MULTIPLE REFERRERS
   If two partners submit the same prospect, Company allocates the fee in good faith by
   contribution, or to the first accepted submission. Company's determination is final.

7. PARTNER OBLIGATIONS & RESTRICTIONS
   Partner will act lawfully and ethically, make no guarantees about the product, and
   not use Company marks except as permitted in writing.

8. NON-SOLICITATION / NON-CIRCUMVENTION
   For [12] months post-termination, neither party solicits the other's employees;
   Company will not circumvent Partner on a deal Partner validly introduced.

9. CONFIDENTIALITY   [or cross-reference the Mutual NDA]

10. TERM & TERMINATION
    Initial term [12] months, auto-renewing. Either party may terminate for convenience
    on [14] days' notice and for cause with a [30]-day cure period. Referrals accepted
    before termination remain payable (the "tail", Section 11).

11. SURVIVAL / TAIL
    Fees on Referrals accepted before termination remain payable for [3–12] months.

12. BOILERPLATE
    Independent contractor; IP ownership stays with each party; indemnification;
    limitation of liability; assignment; governing law [__]; entire agreement; notices.

SCHEDULE A — COMMISSION SCHEDULE
   Rate: [__]%  |  Basis: [first-year ARR]  |  Recurring: [no / __ months]
   Qualifying event: [customer's first paid invoice]  |  Exclusions: [__]
```

**Key negotiation levers:** commission rate & base (one-time vs. recurring; first-year vs. lifetime); length of the eligibility window (longer favors partner); first-transaction-only vs. renewals (biggest lifetime-value swing); tail length.
**Benchmark:** pure referral commonly **10–15% of first-year ARR**; recurring/affiliate-style up to **20–30%**.

## 2. Reseller / Channel Partner Agreement

*Partner resells the product (buys-and-resells or agent-of-record), often owns the customer relationship, prices within bands, sometimes provides first-line support.*

**Document skeleton (body short; detail in exhibits):**
```
1. Parties & recitals
2. Definitions
3. Appointment & license grant     ── grant BEFORE price: resell / sublicense / bundle;
                                       [exclusive/non-exclusive]; buy-resell vs. agent
4. Territory & channels            ── precise geography/vertical; named-account carve-outs
5. Pricing, discount & margin      ── EXHIBIT B: discount off LIST (not fixed $);
                                       price-protection so margin % survives a list cut;
                                       discount bands; currency; tax; rebates; margin floor
6. Pricing authority               ── reseller-set / vendor floor / MSRP
7. Deal registration               ── method; protection window [__ days]; uplift [__%];
                                       extension process; first-to-register rule
8. Order process (order-to-cash)   ── PO/acceptance; who collects payment; vendor due [__ days]
9. Minimum commitments             ── annual minimum $[__]; reporting cadence; # certified reps
                                       (use explicit numbers, not "reasonable efforts")
10. Customer ownership & data      ── who owns the customer & data; what happens on termination
11. Support obligations            ── EXHIBIT C: first-line (reseller) vs. 2nd/3rd (vendor); SLAs
12. End-user terms (EULA flow-down)
13. IP license & branding          ── trademark rules for reselling/marketing
14. Taxes & delivery
15. Reps, warranties, indemnity, liability cap
16. Confidentiality
17. Term, termination & wind-down  ── convenience notice [60–90 days]; for-cause cure;
                                       in-flight customer & renewal transition
18. Boilerplate
EXHIBITS: A Territory | B Pricing/Discount schedule | C SLAs | D Certification requirements
```

**Key negotiation levers:** base discount vs. tier uplift; **exclusivity ↔ minimum commitment** (never grant exclusivity without a hard minimum); deal-reg window & uplift; price protection and margin floor; who owns renewals and customer data.
**Benchmark:** partner margins/discounts commonly **20–40%** (VAR ~25–35%, MSP ~30–40%, distributor ~8–15%).

## 3. Co-Sell / Strategic Alliance Agreement (+ Rules of Engagement)

*Joint selling with a tech/platform partner. Both keep their own paper with the customer; this governs how you collaborate. In practice this is a lightweight master alliance agreement + a Rules-of-Engagement doc, not a heavy contract.*

```
CO-SELL / STRATEGIC ALLIANCE AGREEMENT  (framework)

1. PURPOSE & SCOPE          Joint go-to-market between [Company] and [Partner] to serve
                            mutual customers in [segment/region].
2. DEFINITIONS              Sourced Opportunity, Influenced Opportunity, Joint Account.
3. RULES OF ENGAGEMENT (the core — see one-pager below)
                            Deal leadership defaults; pricing authority stays with each
                            party; technical-support responsibilities; escalation path;
                            response SLA [e.g., logged next step within 48h].
4. LEAD / OPPORTUNITY SHARING   Mechanism [PRM / Crossbeam / shared sheet]; distinguish a
                            lead-share (referral) from true co-sell (one party registers,
                            both work it).
5. ATTRIBUTION & CREDITING  Define Sourced vs. Influenced and how each is credited.
6. JOINT MARKETING & CO-BRANDING   Who produces what; approval rights; trademark use;
                            announcement/PR rights.
7. MARKET DEVELOPMENT FUNDS (MDF)   Pool $[__]; match [__]%; pre-approval; proof-of-performance
                            (partner submits leads the activity generated). [MDF = discretionary
                            pre-approved; distinct from Co-op = % of partner sales.]
8. CONFIDENTIALITY (master, covering all joint customers)
9. DATA SHARING             Account-mapping tool [Crossbeam]; permitted use of shared data.
10. NON-EXCLUSIVITY         Independent businesses; no agency; neither can bind the other.
11. GOVERNANCE              Named alliance leads; QBR cadence; joint scorecard/metrics.
12. TERM & BOILERPLATE
```

**Rules of Engagement — one-pager (put this where both field teams can see it):**
```
WHO LEADS:        [Partner owns the customer relationship by default; Company leads product/technical]
HOW TO ENGAGE:    Register the joint opp in [tool] before first customer meeting
RESPONSE SLA:     Log a next step within 48h; auto-escalate to the partner manager after 24h silence
PRICING:          Each party sets its own price; no discounting the other's product without consent
CONFLICT:         First-to-register on a net-new joint account; disputes → the two alliance leads, not auto-reject
CREDIT:           Sourced = originator; Influenced = materially advanced. Logged at opp creation.
```

**Key levers:** sourced-vs-influenced definitions; MDF pool size & match %; deal-leadership defaults; co-marketing/category exclusivity.
**Benchmark (directional, single-source):** MDF often cited at **2–5% of partner revenue**, ~**50% match** on approved costs.

## 4. Technology / Integration Partnership Agreement

*Governs an API/integration between two products. For an AI company, the data/model-training clause is the one that matters most.*

```
TECHNOLOGY / INTEGRATION PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT  (framework)

1. Parties & recitals
2. Definitions
3. API LICENSE & ACCESS GRANT      What's licensed, to build what; who owns derivative
                                   works/improvements; [sandbox vs. prod]; [sublicensable?]
4. PERMITTED USE, SCOPE & RATE LIMITS   Access scope; rate limits; prohibited uses.
5. DEPRECATION / CHANGE NOTICE     [__] days' notice before breaking API changes.
6. DATA PROCESSING, PRIVACY & SECURITY   ★ AI-CRITICAL ★
      Permitted purposes; prohibited uses; whether shared data may be used to TRAIN MODELS
      or passed to third parties [default: NO]; breach-notification window [__ hrs];
      security standard [SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / PCI-DSS]; attach a DPA where personal data flows.
7. IP OWNERSHIP                    Each keeps background IP; define joint-work ownership;
                                   feedback license.
8. BRANDING / CO-MARKETING         Logo/trademark use; marketplace-listing rights; co-branded assets.
9. SUPPORT                         Who supports the integration; engineering escalation path.
10. REVENUE SHARE (if any)         Often none (mutual value). If present: [__]% of net [base];
                                   put the % in an ADDENDUM so it changes without reopening.
11. WARRANTIES, INDEMNITY (esp. IP infringement), LIABILITY CAP
12. CONFIDENTIALITY
13. TERM & TERMINATION (+ deprecation/transition)
14. BOILERPLATE
```

**Key levers (AI-specific first):** **data-use / no-model-training / third-party-sharing restrictions**; deprecation-notice length & API uptime SLA; integration/category exclusivity; rev-share base (and whether any exists); IP-indemnity scope and liability cap.

## 5. Mutual NDA

*Bilateral confidentiality — standard before any partnership discussion. Watch the **two different clocks**.*

```
MUTUAL NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT

1. PARTIES
2. CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION — definition + carve-outs (public; independently developed;
   lawfully received from a third party; already known).
3. PERMITTED PURPOSE — [evaluating a potential partnership].
4. PERMITTED RECIPIENTS — need-to-know, bound by equivalent terms.
5. OBLIGATIONS — protect; don't disclose; don't use beyond the Purpose.
6. EXCLUSIONS (as in §2).
7. TERM vs. SURVIVAL — TWO CLOCKS, don't conflate:
      Disclosure Term  = how long info may be exchanged      → typical [1–3] years
      Survival Period  = how long obligations continue after → typical [2–5] years
      (common partnership pattern: 2-year disclosure, 3-year survival)
8. TRADE SECRETS — protected indefinitely under applicable law (handle separately).
9. RETURN/DESTRUCTION of materials on request.
10. REMEDIES — injunctive relief; no license granted; no obligation to proceed.
11. GOVERNING LAW; entire agreement; notices.
```
**Drafting pitfalls:** state exactly when survival runs *from* (termination vs. last disclosure); **list which sections survive** rather than relying on implication; keep trade-secret protection perpetual; align the NDA's survival with any MSA it supports.

> **MSA framing:** an MSA is the umbrella commercial contract; partnership economics live in **order forms / SOWs / schedules** beneath it. The NDA can be standalone or folded into the MSA's confidentiality section — specify which controls.

## 6. Partner Program Terms / Rules of Engagement (program document)

*A program-level doc, incorporated by reference into each partner agreement and updatable by you. Covers tiers, benefits, requirements, and deal-registration rules.*

```
[COMPANY] PARTNER PROGRAM GUIDE

1. PROGRAM OVERVIEW & ELIGIBILITY
2. PARTNER TYPES — Referral | Reseller | Technology/ISV | Solution/SI
3. TIERS (requirements + benefits) — see table below
4. DEAL REGISTRATION POLICY — how to register; what qualifies (net-new); protection
   window [__ days]; discount uplift [__%]; conflict resolution (first-to-register;
   disputes to the channel manager); expiration/extension process; approval SLA [24–48h]
5. CERTIFICATION / TRAINING REQUIREMENTS
6. MDF / CO-MARKETING RULES
7. REPORTING & QBR OBLIGATIONS
8. CODE OF CONDUCT
9. PROGRAM CHANGES & TERMINATION OF STATUS (demotion for inactivity)
10. INCORPORATION BY REFERENCE into the partner agreement
```

**Illustrative tier table (sample thresholds — set yours against your own data):**

| | Registered / Silver | Gold | Platinum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual revenue threshold | $50K–$100K | ~$250K | ~$1M |
| Certified staff | 1 | 3 | 6 |
| Other requirements | — | + business plan | + dedicated practice, exec sponsor |
| Margin uplift over base | base | +2–4 pts | +5–8 pts |
| MDF | standard pool | up to ~$10K | dedicated budget (~$50K), priority |
| Deal-reg priority / PM support / early access | low | medium | high |

## Benchmark terms table (directional — cite as "typical range reported by [source]")

| Term | Typical range reported |
|---|---|
| Referral commission (pure) | 10–15% of first-year ARR |
| Referral/affiliate, recurring | ~20–30% recurring (median ~25%) |
| Reseller / VAR commission | 20–30% of first-year ARR |
| MSP commission | 20–35% |
| Reseller gross margin (discount off list) | 20–40% (VAR 25–35%, MSP 30–40%, distributor 8–15%) |
| Deal-reg protection window | 30–90 days common; 90–180 days argued appropriate for SaaS |
| Deal-reg discount uplift | illustrative 20%→30% on registered deals |
| MDF | 2–5% of partner revenue; ~50% match on approved costs |
| NDA disclosure term / survival | 1–3 yr term; 2–5 yr survival |
| Referral tail / survival (commission) | 3–12 months |
| Reseller convenience-termination notice | 60–90 days (integration-heavy) |

> **Benchmark caveat:** no recent survey from a recognized primary source surfaced in research; most figures are vendor blogs/templates without stated methodology. For defensible numbers, pull PartnerStack's "State of Partnerships," Crossbeam's ecosystem reports, or Canalys/Channel Futures data directly.
