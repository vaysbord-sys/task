# PART 1 — The frameworks (your working vocabulary)

### Ideal Partner Profile (IPP)
The partner-side version of an Ideal Customer Profile. Defines the attributes of a partner most likely to succeed with you: **customer-base overlap** with your ICP, **complementary (not competing) product**, **delivery capability**, **economic motivation**, and **cultural fit**. Build it *before* recruiting so you chase fit over volume. A weak IPP is why programs drown in signed-but-dormant partners. Score every prospect against it.

### The Partner Lifecycle
`Recruit → Onboard → Enable → Activate → Manage → Grow` (some models expand to 9–10 stages). *Recruit:* find right-fit partners per the IPP. *Onboard:* portal access, agreements, initial training. *Enable:* give playbooks, co-branded assets, product knowledge. *Activate:* get them to their first registered/sourced deal — **the hardest step; most partners never activate.** *Manage:* cadence, QBRs, governance. *Grow:* expand the winners, sunset the dead. Program health ≈ "what % of partners move from onboarded to activated."

### Partner Motions (name the motion before you design anything)
- **Referral** — partner passes a lead for a fee and steps out. Lowest involvement.
- **Reseller / VAR** — partner buys at a discount and owns the transaction, pricing (within bands), often first-line support.
- **Co-sell** — both companies work the same live deal together. Highest collaboration, highest yield; now the default in cloud-marketplace enterprise deals.
- **Technology / ISV** — a product integration with a shared customer base and joint roadmap; usually no resale mechanics.

Each needs **different contracts, incentives, and metrics**. Mixing them is the #1 cause of live-deal disputes.

### Program Tiers
A hierarchy (e.g., Registered → Silver → Gold → Platinum) that allocates *your finite attention* to partners who produce. Each tier has **entry criteria** (revenue, certifications, co-marketing) and **benefits** (bigger margins, MDF, leads, dedicated support). Keep it simple early — 3 tiers, 5–7 plain-language criteria each mapped to a CRM field. Status is **not permanent** — demote for inactivity. Don't promise tier benefits you can't yet service.

### Rules of Engagement (RoE)
The written rules for who owns a customer/deal and how direct reps and partners (and partners vs. partners) interact, including conflict resolution. **RoE is the trust contract of your ecosystem.** Without it, direct sales and partners collide on the same accounts and partners disengage. Publish it, align sales comp to it, enforce it visibly.

### Deal Registration
The process where a partner formally claims an opportunity; you validate it's net-new and qualified, then grant a **protection window** and better economics. It's the single most important channel-conflict control and the source of clean *sourced* attribution. Works only with a **fast acceptance SLA (24–48h)**, clear net-new criteria, and CRM de-duplication. Slow or arbitrary approvals destroy trust in the whole program.

### Nearbound / Ecosystem-Led Growth (ELG)
The thesis that buyers trust *who* they already know, so the fastest path to revenue is operating *through* partners who surround your buyer. Operationally: use partner **account-overlap data** to (1) source warm intros, (2) accelerate stuck deals via partner insight/intros, (3) expand accounts. It reframes partnerships from a side channel into a GTM layer supporting sales, marketing, and CS. The practical unlock is **data** — map overlaps (via Crossbeam/Reveal) and turn relationships into measurable pipeline.

### QBRs & Joint Business Plans
The **Quarterly Business Review** is the governance cadence with each strategic partner: review pipeline and sourced/influenced revenue against a **joint business plan** (shared goals, named target accounts, agreed plays, enablement commitments, KPIs you both signed). Its purpose is accountability and resource reallocation — each quarter decide to invest more, hold, or sunset. A QBR without a pre-agreed plan is just a status meeting.

### Attribution: Sourced vs. Influenced
- **Partner-SOURCED** = the partner *originated* the opportunity (deal wouldn't exist without them). Binary, evidence-based, fixed at creation.
- **Partner-INFLUENCED** = you originated it; a partner *materially helped advance* it. Broader, judgment-based.
- **The counterfactual test:** "Would this deal still close — but slower or smaller — without the partner? If yes, it's *influenced*, not *sourced*." Keep them in **separate fields**; blending them inflates numbers and destroys CFO/CRO trust.

### MDF vs. Co-op
- **MDF (Market Development Funds)** — discretionary vendor money for *partner* marketing, **pre-approved** against a plan, paid on proof of performance.
- **Co-op** — earned as a **% of partner sales**. Tie access to a balanced give/get ratio.

### ROI metrics (what justifies the function)
Partner-sourced revenue/pipeline; partner-influenced revenue (reported *separately*); **activation rate** (% of partners producing); **time-to-first-deal**; win-rate/attach lift on partner-involved deals; co-sell cycle time vs. direct; marketplace-sourced revenue; MDF/program ROI. The maturity signal is moving from **activity** metrics (partners recruited, MDF spent) to **outcome** metrics (sourced revenue, activation rate). Pick 3–4 outcome metrics and defend the budget with them.

### GoToEcosystem (Allan Adler)
The idea that "GoToMarket" is being replaced by orchestrating the *web of partners around each customer* — and that this is a C-suite alignment problem, not a partner-team task.

# PART 2 — The 22 synthesized core lessons

Distilled from across the podcasts and thought leaders, grouped by the parts of your job. These are the repeated, teachable principles.

### Building a program from zero
1. **Start with one revenue-producing play, not a portfolio.** Pick a single partner type and a single motion (e.g., co-sell with one services partner), prove it produces pipeline, then templatize. *(Nearbound, Howdy Partners, PARTNERNOMICS)*
2. **Get executive sponsorship and sales-comp alignment before recruiting.** Programs die from internal misalignment, not partner quality — if reps aren't paid/credited for partner deals, the channel stalls. *(Nearbound #148, Inside Partnering)*
3. **Pick one north-star early: partner-sourced + partner-influenced pipeline/revenue.** Report outcomes, not activity. *(Howdy Partners, GTMnow)*
4. **Validate cheaply before scaling headcount** — a fractional leader or a single play can de-risk the investment case. *(Howdy Partners)*
5. **Partners accelerate what already works; they don't fix what's broken.** Prove a partner hypothesis with a few real partners before building program machinery. *(Partner Standard)*

### Recruiting & activating partners
6. **Fit over volume — qualification is the weakest point in most programs.** The default failure is approving every applicant. Score against the IPP. *(Magentrix, Introw)*
7. **Over-invest in the few.** A small subset of partners drives most results; concentrate rather than sprawl across a bloated roster. *(Nearbound #114)*
8. **Re-activate before you recruit.** Energy spent re-engaging dormant existing partners usually beats chasing net-new logos. *(Nearbound #114)*
9. **Reduce friction to near-zero.** Make referring/registering a deal a single form or Slack message; every added step halves participation. *(PARTNERNOMICS)*
10. **Enable internally first, externally second.** Your own reps must know when and how to pull a partner in before partner enablement matters. *(Howdy Partners #76)*
11. **Build a "first 90 days" partner path:** what we do, how we co-sell, how to register, what's in it for them. *(Howdy Partners #76)*
12. **The enrollment→activation gap is where programs leak.** Fix the stage with the steepest drop-off first; low activation despite good onboarding almost always traces back to recruiting the wrong partners. *(Magentrix, Introw)*

### Co-sell motions
13. **The highest-leverage artifact is a shared account map / co-sell pipeline.** Overlap data (who you both sell to) is where partner pipeline actually comes from. *(Ecosystem Aces, Crossbeam)*
14. **Co-sell is rep-to-rep trust, manufactured at scale.** The program's job is to engineer personal trust between your sellers and partner sellers. *(Friends with Benefits)*
15. **For enterprise/hyperscaler co-sell, align to the partner's field incentives and marketplace mechanics** (marketplace private offers, their co-sell programs). You win by making *their* seller look good. *(Inside Partnering / Ecosystem Aces)*
16. **Buyers trust the ecosystem before they trust you** — roughly two-thirds of the journey happens via third parties, so position partners as the trusted first touch. *(Nearbound #153, McBain)*
17. **Don't single-thread through the partner.** The partner's one champion is a single point of failure, same as in direct sales. *(30MPC + Nearbound)*

### Negotiation (partner terms, deal splits, internal resourcing)
18. **Use tactical empathy and labeling** ("It seems like protecting your services margin is the priority") to defuse tension and surface the real interest before bargaining. *(Chris Voss)*
19. **Ask calibrated "How/What" questions** to shift problem-solving onto the other side without saying "no," and aim for **"No"-safe questions** to lower their guard. *(Chris Voss)*
20. **Run an accusation audit** — name their likely objections first ("You probably think we'll favor our direct team") to take the sting out. *(Chris Voss)*
21. **Lead with compassionate curiosity, not persuasion.** You rarely change a stubborn counterpart; you reframe so they choose your outcome. Separate the person from the problem. *(Negotiate Anything)*

### Partner–direct sales alignment (the make-or-break)
22. **Eliminate channel conflict by design, not after the fight:** clear deal-registration rules, territory/account rules, and crediting so a partner deal *adds to* a rep's number instead of threatening it — and **compensate direct reps for supporting (not competing with) partner deals.** Partnerships must plug into the core GTM narrative and *share a number* with sales/marketing; a parallel, isolated partner motion gets starved. *(The Transaction, Nearbound #148, Friends with Benefits)*

# PART 3 — The negotiation playbook (applied to partnerships)

You negotiate constantly: partner commercial terms, revenue splits, internal resourcing, and comp fights with sales. Use **tactical empathy** (Voss) and **compassionate curiosity** (Christian), not positional haggling.

### The six moves

1. **Accusation audit (open with it).** Pre-empt their objections out loud.
   > "You probably think we're going to favor our direct team and hand you the scraps. You may also think our margins are too thin to make this worth your while."
   Naming the fear defuses it.

2. **Labeling.** Name the emotion/interest you detect, starting "It seems like…" / "It sounds like…".
   > "It seems like protecting your implementation-services revenue matters more here than the headline discount."
   This makes the other side feel understood and surfaces the real interest.

3. **Mirroring.** Repeat their last 1–3 words as a question, then stay silent ~4 seconds.
   > Partner: "The deal-reg window is too short." You: "Too short?" *(silence)* — and they elaborate on what they actually need.

4. **Calibrated "How/What" questions.** Shift problem-solving onto them without saying "no."
   > "How am I supposed to offer 40% margin and still fund the co-marketing you're asking for?"
   It gets them designing a workable deal with you.

5. **Go for "No," not "Yes."** "No" makes people feel safe and in control.
   > "Is it a bad idea to start with a 90-day co-sell pilot before we commit to annual minimums?"

6. **"That's right" > "You're right."** Summarize their worldview until they say *"that's right"* — the signal they feel genuinely understood, which is when agreement becomes possible. ("You're right" is often just a brush-off.)

### Worked example — reseller wants 40%, you can give 25%
1. *Accusation audit:* "You probably think 25% is us lowballing you and that we don't value the work you put in."
2. *Label:* "It sounds like the number has to cover real delivery cost, not just referral effort."
3. *Calibrated question:* "What would the margin need to protect for this to be worth building a practice around?"
4. *Reframe to total economics:* trade headline margin for things that cost you less than cash — deal-reg protection, qualified leads, MDF, co-marketing, early product access, a renewal/upsell commission tail.
5. *Tier it:* 25% base, uplift to 30%+ at Gold on proven volume — so the higher number is *earned*, aligning their incentive with production.
6. *Go for "No":* "Is it crazy to lock 25% now with a written path to 30% at $250K, rather than stall the whole deal over 15 points today?"

> The lesson that threads through all of it: **negotiate the whole economic relationship (leads, MDF, protection, renewals, early access), not the single margin number.** That's how you turn a zero-sum fight into a bigger pie.
