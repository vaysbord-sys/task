01 · The brief, distilled

What Moose is actually asking for

This is not an interior refresh. It is a brand world that can scale to 20 stores. Its job is emotional: people leave in a better mood than they arrived in.

The job
Lift the mood on entry. The room should feel exciting, happy, playful, cozy and energized. People should come for the feeling, and the dessert is the reason to walk in.
The model
La La Land's yellow. One color carries the emotion. He is not asking to copy the film's look. Laza needs its own version of that feeling.
Color
A lighter, happier blue to replace the heavy royal blue. He liked the blue in the tennis project. He is not locked to a shade and trusts the studio to choose it and the full palette.
Materials
Oak for warmth, chrome for youth. Tile is also welcome. The studio chooses the materials.
Social
A 20–30 minute visit, not a laptop café. Closer seating, movement, music, day-club energy without looking like a club. The references are Mimi's and Yo-Chi. People should feel like they are somewhere.
Photo
An 8–9/10 on Instagram without trying. The moments should come from architecture, color, packaging, light and product. One major WOW moment at the entrance that can become part of the brand.
Price feel
High design, accessible. Premium but never luxury, and never cheap.
Audience
Styled for 20–30-year-olds, welcoming to everyone. Families, teens and older guests should all feel at home.
System
Every touchpoint from one personality: cups, packaging, uniforms, menus, signage, phrases, music, furniture, social and stores. Laza needs owned elements that work without the logo.
Persona
The person everyone wants to be around. Happy, confident, playful and social, without trying hard to be cool. They make you feel seen, and 20 minutes with them fixes a bad day.

Insight from research

Laza is a night brand

Both stores are open 2pm to 2am. The busiest hours are after dark, so the blue has to feel happy under evening light, not only in a daylight render. This shaped both directions.

Insight from research

Kunafa is crowded

The "Dubai chocolate" wave made pistachio green with gold the default kunafa look. Laza already has the product. It needs a look nobody else in the category is using.

Insight from research

@lazacafe and @laza.cafe each show roughly 130K followers. Consolidating them is a quick win for the rebrand launch.

Researcher · slop filter

Five things neither direction is allowed to do

  1. Neon script signs, flower walls or pink "photo walls" built for the camera.
  2. Pistachio green with gold foil. That's the Dubai-chocolate look and already the kunafa default.
  3. Generic arabesque stars, calligraphy-style fonts used as flavor, crowns, lanterns or "royal" gold.
  4. Soft pastel gradient, rounded sans and nothing else. That's the default 2025 café rebrand.
  5. Tiffany or baby-blue pulled off a swatch card. Our blue needs a specific hue, a name and a warm partner.

We also dropped the arch shape, which is the most overused café device of 2021 to 2025.

Direction A

Blue
Hour.

The idea. La La Land owns golden hour. Laza owns the blue hour, the stretch after sunset when the sky goes soft blue, the streetlights come on and the night is still ahead. That's exactly when Laza is busiest. We take one clear, airy blue and put it on the ceiling, the cup, the uniform and the feed, so the whole brand feels like a good night about to start.

Line: "Stay for the good part."

Logo · "The Rise"A.1

A scoop, a sun and a smile in one shape

The mark is a circle rising over a curve. It reads as a scoop in a cup, a sun coming up over the horizon, and a smile. That makes the mood-lift promise literal. It's simple enough to draw from memory and to cut from chrome.

The wordmark is a heavy, slightly condensed lowercase: confident and friendly without going childish. Custom-draw the final version. The z gets a soft-serve curl at its terminal as the only flourish.

Owned without the logo: the Rise shape as door pulls, lid emboss, pastry stencil and table inlays.

Space · the WOW is the ceilingA.4

The Sky

A continuous barrel-vault ceiling in lacquered Laza Sky, lit from a hidden cove. The cove is programmed to the store's hours:

  • 2pm Clear daylight blue, bright and fresh
  • 8pm Blue hour. Sky deepens and Streetlamp sconces warm up
  • 12am–2am Deep blue with a warm glow at table height

The WOW is the room itself, and it looks different every time you visit. It photographs naturally, needs no sign, and works in any footprint as a signature for store 20.

Materials + social layout

  • Mirror-chrome bar front that reflects the Sky. The youthful note Moose asked for.
  • Oak "bleachers": three wide steps of social seating along one wall, like a day club or tennis stands. People sit close, face the room and watch the flow.
  • High oak rail at the window for 10-minute visits.
  • Sky glazed tile floor band leading from the door to the counter, so the path is the brand.
  • Terry and chrome stools, a nod to the tennis project.
  • Music: Arabic disco and funk (the Habibi Funk catalog) into current R&B and house. Volume rises after 9pm.

VoiceA.6

Good mood, open till 2. Stay for the good part. Tonight's forecast: kunafa. Bad day? Sit down. It's always blue hour somewhere. Here.

Direction B

Ahlan.
أهلاً

The idea. In the Levant, you're welcomed before you're served. Direction B takes the loud, bustling sweet shop of Nablus and Damascus, with trays in the window, strangers talking across counters and kunafa flipped in front of you, and rebuilds it for Bay Ridge and Astoria at 1am. The persona Moose described, "they make you feel seen", is Arabic hospitality. Here that is the brand.

Line: "Ahlan. Come as you are, leave sweeter."

Logo · "The Tray"B.1

The kunafa tray is the shape

Every Levantine sweet shop has one: the round, fluted copper tray (seniyeh) that kunafa is baked and flipped in. Its scalloped edge becomes Laza's badge and, more importantly, its system. It shows up on lids, table edges, the awning fringe, menu borders and receipt tear-offs.

The lockup is bilingual at equal weight. Arabic isn't decoration here. It's half the name. The Latin is a warm, soft serif. The Arabic is Lalezar, a joyful display face, so the pair reads fun rather than formal.

Name check: we're assuming Laza comes from لذّة (ladhdha, "delight" or "pleasure"). Confirm with Moose. If true, it's the brand's best story.

Space · the WOW is a ritualB.4

The Pull Bar

A long, open kunafa counter in Tile Blue glazed tile with a chrome top, set right at the entrance. Trays come out of the oven, get flipped, and the cheese is pulled under a single warm spotlight, in front of you. Behind it, a slowly rotating chrome turntable of whole trays sits in the window, a modern version of the Nablus evening window.

This is the Media.Monks lesson: one hero object, a stretching cheese pull, turned into the thing people film. It's content that is real product, not decoration, so it can't date.

Materials + social layout

  • Handmade glazed tile in Tile Blue, laid in a simple stacked grid. Texture comes from the glaze, not from a pattern.
  • Long shared oak tables (sofra) down the middle. Strangers sit near each other, which is how the conversation starts.
  • Built-in perimeter bench (majlis) with Rose Syrup cushions. The whole room faces inward.
  • Scalloped chrome edges on tables and the awning, pulled from the logo.
  • Warm globe pendants that pool light on the tables, so the blue glows and never goes cold.
  • Music: current Arab pop and Khaleeji into Afrobeats and R&B. Host greets every guest with "ahlan" at the door.

Our recommendation

Lead with A, and bring in B's two best assets.

Blue Hour answers the brief's central ask most directly: a lighter blue that owns a feeling the way yellow does for La La Land. It also scales to 20 stores and every audience. It's missing a ritual, and B has the best one. Take the Pull Bar (clad in Sky tile with a chrome top) and the equal-weight bilingual lockup into A. That gives you a color people feel, a ritual people film, and a name with a real story.

If Moose wants the heritage to be the headline rather than a layer, present B as a full alternative. It's the braver and more distinctive route.

05 · Handoff

What the studio does next

Questions for Moose before round 2

  1. Does Laza come from لذّة ("delight")? If yes, the name becomes the brand story in both directions.
  2. Which "Yochi"? We found Yo-Chi, the Australian chain. Is that the one he meant?
  3. Which tennis-project blue? Send the hex or a photo, and we'll test it against Laza Sky.
  4. Which Instagram handle is official? @lazacafe or @laza.cafe. We'll plan to consolidate at launch.
  5. Is "Laza by Chocolate Factory" on Apple Maps related, and is the 2am close the same at every future location?

Round 2 deliverables

  1. Custom wordmark for the chosen route: Latin and Arabic drawn together, not a set font.
  2. Blue test on real material: lacquer, glazed tile and printed cup, under 2700K and 4000K light at night.
  3. Photoreal renders: entrance WOW, counter and seating, at 3pm and 11pm.
  4. Packaging dummies: cup family, kunafa box, bag, sticker sheet.
  5. Voice and music guide: 30 lines and 3 playlists by daypart.
  6. Kit of parts: which 6 elements every store must have, so store 20 still feels like Laza.
