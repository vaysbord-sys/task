## Patterns
- Sentence length: short declaratives or one long sensory list. Levain and VL run 5-15 words. Salt & Straw runs one 15-20 word ingredient sentence.
- Tone: warm and confident, never salesy. It reads as a person talking, not a campaign.
- Humor: light and understated ("adored by tea lovers and haters alike"). It comes from contrast, not puns or exclamation marks.
- Naming: literal ingredient names (Honey Lavender, Earl Grey Tea, Chocolate Chip Walnut). No invented cute names. Quirk lives in the pairing, not the label.
- Person: "we" for making ("We steep pounds..."). "You" is rare and implied. The brand talks about its process, not at the customer.
- Place: specificity gives credibility (Oregon Bartlett pears, Yunnan, Guatemalan fleur de sel, Upper West Side, yellow truck). Provenance is named, not gestured at.
- Sensory words: concrete textures (rich, gooey, molten, toasty, crunchy, cave-aged, herbaceous). Verbs are physical: steep, fold, spike.
- Rhythm and ritual: drops, weekly cadence, pink box, party. The occasion is part of the product.

## Avoid (these brands never write this)
- "Indulge in a symphony of flavors" or "elevate your dessert experience"
- "Crafted with love and passion" or "artisanal goodness" with no named ingredient
- "Where tradition meets innovation" (any "X meets Y" tagline)
- Triple adjective stacks such as "luscious, decadent, irresistible," and rhetorical "Why settle for ordinary?"
- Em-dash reveals and "It's not just dessert, it's an experience"

## 12 LAZA line candidates
**Earthy (sun-baked, craft)**
1. Kunafa, baked until the edges go the color of late afternoon.
2. We pull it from the oven when it's ready, not when the timer says.
3. Pistachio, honey, butter. Nothing you need a footnote for.

**Arabic / Oriental (hospitality, Levantine)**
4. Sit. Someone is bringing you something warm.
5. Our kunafa, the way a grandmother would insist on it.
6. Rose water, orange blossom, a second plate whether you asked or not.

**Ultramarine + Violet (late-night, vivid)**
7. Open till 2am, because sugar hits differently after midnight.
8. Matcha at 1:40am. We're not judging.
9. The lights go violet at eleven. The crepes get better.

**Blue (bright, happy)**
10. Cold froyo, hot kunafa. Pick a side or take both.
11. Brooklyn's sweetest afternoon starts at 2.
12. Bring a friend. Split nothing.
