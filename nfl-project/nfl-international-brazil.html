02 — THE MARKET

WHY BRAZIL
WHY NOW

On September 27, 2026, the Ravens play the Cowboys at the Maracanã — the NFL's first regular-season game in Rio, with a minimum of three games committed over five years. A game creates a spike. What turns a spike into a market is what happens the other 364 days.

03 · The payoff

O PRÓXIMO

The first Brazilian in the Hall of Fame. A hero a whole country claims. That is what makes a market self-sustaining — not tickets, a face.

This isn't a proposal for something new — it's the creative system on top of what the NFL is already building: NFL Flag Brazil runs in 80 schools, trains PE coaches to NFL standard, and just held the country's first U-15 girls' championship. It has everything except one unifying idea.

03 — THE BRIDGE

A COUNTRY THAT
ALREADY PLAYS

Brazil is jogo bonito — the beautiful game, barefoot, on sand and in the street. American football looks like the opposite: armor, rules, stoppages. The bridge is the body. Every instinct Brazil already has is a football skill waiting for a new sport.

FUTSAL FOOTWORK→ROUTE RUNNING

The cut is the juke

The tight, quick-twitch footwork that makes Brazilian futsal players magic is exactly what separates a receiver getting open.

CAPOEIRA→THE SPIN MOVE

Evasion as art

A culture that turned dodging into dance already understands the most beautiful thing a ball-carrier can do: not get touched.

THE BEACH→THE FIELD

Copacabana is a stadium

Brazil plays on sand by default. Flag football needs no turf, no pads, no gates — just a stretch of beach and a belt.

CARNIVAL→GAMEDAY

Spectacle is native

No country stages collective joy better. The NFL's gameday theatre isn't foreign here — it's a Tuesday.

01

Cairo Santos — the proof it's already happening

Born in São Paulo, he played soccer before he ever kicked a football — and became the first Brazilian-born player in NFL history. One person, the whole soccer-to-football bridge. There are 36 million fans behind him and no icon yet. That's the opening.

04 — THE SYSTEM

THE SHIELD

Take the NFL apart into its icons — the helmet, the ball, the goalpost, the turf, the whistle, the cleats. Cut each one out, intensify it, lay it on the map. Then recolor the language for Brazil: a new ball, a new kit, a stadium reborn in the city. We don't touch the shield — we translate it.
