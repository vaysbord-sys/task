## Part 0 — Sharp start

**The enterprise buyer.** The VP or Head of Creative / Brand Studio at a high-volume, regulated advertiser — first, US sportsbooks and prediction markets. The CMO holds the budget; Legal/Compliance and Procurement/Security hold the veto. They own a performance-creative budget that is rising: DraftKings' sales and marketing expense was $1.38B in FY2025 and grew 38% in Q2 2026 while revenue fell 5% ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001883685/000188368526000029/dkng-20260630.htm)).

**The one pain a consumer tool can't solve: compliant variants at volume.** Every promo ships in up to 31 state versions plus DC — New Jersey requires 1-800-GAMBLER, Connecticut (888) 789-7777, Massachusetts a helpline in state-set wording that cannot be obscured ([205 CMR 256](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/205-CMR-25600-sports-wagering-advertising)) — and everyone on screen must be 21+ under the AGA code. Operators now also run a second regime: FanDuel Predicts trades sports in 18 states where FanDuel has no sportsbook ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/01/15/fanduel-predicts-all-50-states/)). A consumer tool makes a good clip. It cannot make the 50th variant match the first, keep characters identical across cuts, and leave a trail Legal will sign off on game-day deadlines.

**The riskiest assumption, in one sentence:** that brand-safety and legal teams will approve Higgsfield-made video for paid media before we can show attested trust — a SOC 2 report rather than "SOC 2-aligned" controls, per-model indemnity and a clean brand-safety record — in the year of Forbes' February coverage of Higgsfield and Kalshi's September AI-ad backlash.

Aggregation alone no longer differentiates: Adobe Firefly carries 30+ models ([Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/03/19/adobe-firefly-expands-video-image-creation-with-new-ai-capabilities-custom-models)) and Runway added Veo, Kling and Seedance in 2026 ([Runway](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/51248305153683-Enterprise-FAQ-Third-party-Models-in-Runway)). Three points that survive a procurement call:

1. **Continuity.** The Sora API shut down on September 24, 2026 and the Disney–OpenAI deal collapsed with it. Teams built on one model lost their workflow. On Higgsfield the brand kit, characters and approvals survive any model's exit — the job re-routes.
2. **Speed to frontier.** The #1 model on Artificial Analysis changed about 8 times in 17 months. A single-model contract locks you to last quarter's quality; Higgsfield integrates new frontier models within \~24 hours (company claim).
3. **Cost.** At Kalshi's \~4–5% keep rate, iterating on stills and $0.04–0.10/s models and finishing on Veo 3.1 at $0.40/s cuts generation cost several-fold versus running every attempt on the premium model (Part 3).

Where it backfires:

| Counter from a single-model vendor | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| "Depth beats breadth" — model and tools co-designed (Runway Aleph, Act-Two) | Our depth is the workflow layer — Soul ID, Cinema Studio camera control, Supercomputer pipelines — plus our own models (Soul) |
| "Jack of all trades — output is inconsistent across models" | Soul ID references hold characters across models; we publish a recommended model per job |
| "You depend on providers who can cut you off" (Gen-4.5 is licensed to no one) | Multi-sourcing is the hedge: when Sora exited, our customers re-routed the same day; single-model customers started over |
| "Nobody indemnifies third-party models" | Indemnity mapped per model; legal-sensitive assets routed to indemnified paths (Veo on Vertex); a licensed-data option for legal-veto accounts |

## Part 3 — GPU and unit economics

A usable 30-second ad costs about $1.7K–$33K with generative video (base \~$7.8K) versus $50K–$500K for a national spot. Generation is only 5–12% of that cost; people and iteration are the rest. So we sell keep-rate and throughput, not GPU-seconds.

### A. Cost per generation

Cost per usable shot = seconds billed per attempt × price per second × generations to a keeper.

| Driver | Range | Swing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model tier and audio | $0.04/s (MiniMax H3 Max) → $0.40/s (Veo 3.1, 1080p, audio) → $0.60/s (Veo 4K) ([Vertex pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing)) | \~10–15x |
| Resolution | Veo 3.1 Fast: $0.10 (720p) → $0.12 (1080p) → $0.30/s (4K) | 3x |
| Clip length | 5–8 s billed per attempt (Veo base clip is 8 s) | linear |
| Generations to a keeper | Kalshi: 300–400 generations for 15 usable clips ≈ 20–27 per shot ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-ad-kalshi-nba-finals/)) | largest |

**One usable 30-second ad (15 shots)**

| Input | Low | Base | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generations per keeper | 10 | 20 | 40 |
| Seconds billed per attempt | 5 | 8 | 8 |
| Price per second | $0.12 (Veo Fast 1080p) | $0.40 (Veo Std 1080p) | $0.60 (Veo 4K) |
| **Generation** | **$90** | **$960** | **$2,880** |
| People (editor/director at $100–125/h) | $1.6K (1 × 2 days) | $4.8K (2 × 3 days) | $20K (4 × 5 days) |
| Music, VO, legal review | $0 | $2K | $10K |
| **Total** | **≈ $1.7K** | **≈ $7.8K** | **≈ $33K** |

Assumptions are mine except the Kalshi ratio and list prices. **Image-first routing** cuts the base generation line from $960 to \~$240: iterate on stills, animate only approved frames (assumption: 1 keeper in 5 animations), finish on Veo 3.1 Standard.

**The buyer's ROI slide.** National 30-second production: $50K–$500K (2026 vendor estimates); the last industry survey average was $354K (4A's, 2011 data — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/164727/4as-costs-rose-for-spots-agency-markup-came-down.html)). Kalshi's NBA Finals ad: \~$2,000 and two days; Kalshi's CEO put a comparable shoot at "seven figures". The base case is \~45x cheaper than $354K. Against a $10–50K social spot, the high case is not clearly cheaper — so for a sportsbook the ROI is the variant set: once hero shots are approved, each extra state or channel variant costs \~$60 (assumption: two regenerated shots on Veo Fast plus 30 minutes of editing).

### B. Infrastructure options

|  | Shared cloud | Dedicated GPU capacity | Private VPC / on-prem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Lowest; pay per use | Committed; B200 index \~$7.45/GPU-hr on demand ([Ornn](https://data.ornn.com/)), less reserved | Highest: $370–562K per 8×B200 system plus ops ([gpubattle](https://gpubattle.com/guides/nvidia-b200-price)) |
| Latency and throughput | Variable at peaks | Guaranteed lanes for game-day spikes | Fixed by what you buy |
| Security and compliance | Enterprise controls: SSO, RBAC, no-training contract | Same, plus isolation | Maximum data control |
| Ops burden | None | Low (Higgsfield-run) | High (customer ML team) |
| Upgrade path when models refresh | New models on day zero | Day zero | Open-weight models only; each upgrade is a migration. Veo and Kling cannot run on-prem |

**Position:** lead with shared cloud plus enterprise controls. Switch to dedicated capacity when the buyer needs a peak-window SLA (NFL Sundays, NBA nights) or commits enough volume that reserved GPUs are cheaper. Offer private VPC only for a data-residency mandate, and only for the open-weight slice of the work.

### C. The objections, verbatim

**"Why don't we just run open-source models on our own GPUs?"**

"You can, and for some jobs you should — here is the full bill. Capex: an 8-GPU H100 server runs $300–400K, sized for your Sunday peak and idle on Tuesday; rented, an H100 is about $2.65–3.49 an hour today. Quality: the best freely licensed open model trails Veo 3.1 by roughly 30–35 Elo on the with-audio leaderboard, and the strongest open-weight model, MiniMax H3, excludes US, EU and UK use in its license. Payroll: a three-person ML and infra team is about $1.1M a year fully loaded — that alone buys about 2.75 million seconds of Veo 3.1 output. Cadence: the #1 model changed eight times in 17 months; each change is a migration project for you and a dropdown for us. Self-hosted Wan 2.2 lands at about $0.09–0.14 per second — the price of Veo Fast at lower quality. Start with frontier quality on day one; if you have a residency mandate, we scope a private open-weight deployment for that slice."

**"What happens to our price when GPU costs drop?"**

"Two honest points. First, GPU prices don't only fall: H100 rental dropped about 64% from 2024 to mid-2025, rose about 38% between October 2025 and March 2026, and B200 rental jumped 79% in three months to September 2026. Second, you don't pay for GPU-hours — you pay per output, and model prices fall faster than hardware: Veo 3.1 Lite is $0.05 a second against $0.40 for Standard, and Runway cut Gen-4.5 from 25 to 12 credits a second this year. So the contract does two things: your committed rate steps down when our list price does, and every quarter we re-route your workload to the cheapest model that passes your brief. You pay for usable assets; we absorb the hardware volatility."

Sources for the figures in both answers: GPU prices ([Ornn](https://data.ornn.com/), [gpusmith](https://gpusmith.com/articles/en/h100-rental-price-history-trends), [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/nvidia-b200-cloud-price-79-percent-surge-2026/)), engineer pay ([Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/t/software-engineer/title/machine-learning-engineer)), open-weight leaderboard ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video/open-weights)).
